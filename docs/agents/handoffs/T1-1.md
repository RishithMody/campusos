## 1. Completed work

`contracts/` is published: five contracts, eleven example fixtures, and the architecture
document that explains the shape behind them. T2, T3, T4, T5 and T9 can now be built in
parallel from sessions that share no memory.

The load-bearing piece is **`contracts/availability.schema.json`**. It is simultaneously the
Cubbie Skill's output (Track 1), the body of an agent-to-agent proposal (Track 2), and what the
group-chat assistant posts (Track 3). One envelope across three surfaces is what makes this one
product rather than three applications.

**Disclosure is enforced structurally, not by convention.** The schema's conditional rules reject
a `free_busy` envelope that carries a `label` or a `category`, and reject any slot at all at
level `none`. "busy 2-4pm" is representable; "therapy at 2pm" is not, below `full`. A buggy or
malicious client cannot produce an over-disclosing envelope that parses. The runtime filter in
the privacy service is the primary guarantee; the schema is an independent second net.

Three further decisions worth a reviewer's attention:

- **`reason_code` is a closed enum, never prose.** Free-text explanation is the most common way
  an otherwise careful system leaks. `conflict` is routable; "I have therapy at 2" is a breach.
- **`student_ref` is a per-negotiation pseudonym** matching `^stu_[A-Za-z0-9]{16,64}$`. A stable
  cross-negotiation id would let a recipient join two negotiations and rebuild a calendar the
  student never agreed to share.
- **`max_rounds` is capped at 20 in the schema.** An unbounded agent loop is both a cost risk and
  a way for a real disagreement never to reach the humans who could settle it.

## 5. Known limitations

- **`category` leaks more than it appears to.** Knowing someone is `personal`-busy every Tuesday
  at 2 is itself a signal. `free_busy` is the default for that reason, but nothing stops a
  student choosing `category` and being correlated over time.
- **`student_ref` is per-negotiation, but timing still correlates.** Two negotiations with the
  same counterpart can be linked by when they happen. Not solved; out of scope.
- **The ladder is coarse** — no per-recipient level within one negotiation.
- **The OpenAPI document is parsed, not linted.** `validate_contracts.py` checks it is valid YAML
  with `openapi`/`info`/`paths`. It does not run a full OpenAPI 3.1 spec validator, so a
  structurally legal but semantically wrong document would pass. A reviewer should read it.
- **Fixture coverage is per-schema, not exhaustive.** Four negatives target the privacy
  properties specifically; they are not a full boundary-value suite.
- **`api.openapi.yaml` `$ref`s the JSON Schemas by `$id`.** Tooling that does not resolve those
  will need a bundling step. T5 generates types from these files and may hit it first.

## 6. Decisions and assumptions

- **Disclosure enforced server-side on egress, in exactly one place.** Not in agents (LLM-driven
  components cannot be the last line of defence) and not in clients (a rendering bug would become
  a breach). Another architect could have distributed it; I think that is a mistake and said so
  in `docs/architecture.md` §2.
- **Four levels, not more.** `none`/`free_busy`/`category`/`full` covers the demo. Each extra
  level is one more thing four agents implement and QA tests.
- **The category enum is deliberately blunt** — `personal` absorbs health, therapy and family.
  Documented in the schema so nobody "improves" it by adding `medical`.
- **`v1` in the server path, `schema_version` in every document.** Additive versioning from the
  start, so a late change does not break agents mid-flight.
- **Assumption:** 20 is a sane `max_rounds` ceiling. Picked for the demo, not measured. T4 may
  find convergence needs fewer; lowering it later is additive-safe, raising it is not.
- **Assumption:** `format: date-time` is advisory. Most validators do not enforce it by default,
  so T2 must validate timestamps itself rather than trusting the schema.
